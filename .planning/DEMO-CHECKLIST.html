Photo Tips for Best Extraction

Compliance plate = engine bay firewall For trucks: open the bonnet and photograph the metal plate riveted to the flat firewall panel. This has VIN, GVM, compliance date.
Engine number = engine ID plate/sticker Separate from the build plate. On Cummins: metal dataplate on block near injection pump. Take a dedicated close-up — the app will read it into the Engine Number field.
Odometer: always the big number, not trip meter If you see two readings, the larger one (e.g. 186,450km) is the total odometer. The small one resets — ignore it.
Hourmeter: 5-digit reading = almost always wrong If extraction shows over 25,000 hours, it misread the display. The last digit is likely a decimal/tenths display. Re-enter manually.
Multiple plates: machine plate vs component plate Wheel loaders have a bucket plate (capacity in m³, pin dims). Trucks have an engine plate. The app now knows which is which — but if it gets the make wrong, it may have picked the component plate.
Service history: photograph the logbook If a service book or stamp is present, include a photo — the app will extract it as 'Full Service History — Log Books'.
