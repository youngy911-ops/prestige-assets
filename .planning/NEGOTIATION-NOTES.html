If They Ask You Questions
"What do you want out of this?"
"I want the team to stop spending 10 minutes typing up data that a photo can give them in 20 seconds. The Salesforce connection is the last step — right now there's still copy-pasting. I want to close that gap."
Keep it about Slattery. Not about you personally. If they push: "I'm happy to have that conversation separately — today I just want the IT access."
"What if they offer to buy it outright?"
"I appreciate that — let's get the API sorted first and then have a separate conversation about how it's structured going forward. I'm open to whatever makes sense for Slattery."
Don't commit to anything in the room. Don't name a price. "Let me think about that and follow up" is always fine.
"What's your coding background?"
"I have some experience coding but I can't write it in my sleep. I know basic code. What I do know is this codebase inside out — I built every part of it. I work with AI development tools to build and maintain it, and I know the business well enough to keep improving it."
Don't volunteer your limitations. Answer what's asked. You built working software — that's inarguable.
"Can you keep working on this on your current salary?"
"I'm happy to keep going — but the workload is changing significantly and I'd want to make sure my role and package reflects that. Happy to have that conversation separately."
Not yes. Not no. Kicks it to a proper negotiation. Don't get locked in at $70k for developer work.
Salary & Role — Separate Conversation (Not Today)
What you're worth
Current: $70k yardhand. Market for someone who owns a production application they built: $90-100k developer range. You have two years of broken promises on career progression. You have leverage.
Aim for $90-100k. Don't open at $85k — give yourself room. They'd pay an external agency $150-200/hr to build what you built.
Monthly fee / per-booking idea
Monthly is cleaner than per-booking. Frame it as a technology/maintenance fee.
"I'd want a monthly technology fee for the ongoing running and development of the platform — the infrastructure, API costs, and maintenance time are real costs."
Even $500-1,000/month is an extra $6-12k/year and easy for them to justify as a software cost.
Reimbursement for what you've spent
Add up: server costs, OpenAI/Claude credits, domain. Have a number ready.
"I've been self-funding everything — the server, domain, API credits — which has cost me around $X. I'd want that covered, and going forward those costs would be on Slattery."
MacBook
"If I'm going to be working on this properly, I'd need a development machine. A MacBook is the standard — that's what I'd need to do this right."
Completely normal request for a developer role. Don't apologise for asking.
On two years of broken promises
You don't have to say this — but if you need to, you can.
"I've been here two years, I was told there'd be progression, and I've just built something that saves the company tens of thousands in labour. I need the package to reflect that."
Then let it sit. Don't fill the silence.
On IP / can they take the app
Possible they have a claim if the IP clause in your contract covers work-related to your employer's business. Don't raise this today. Your leverage is being the only person who can maintain it.
If this conversation happens: "Happy to work through the details — today I just want to get moving on the Salesforce connection." Never a good idea to negotiate IP in a room full of people.
Know your number before any negotiation
What salary would you need to leave the yard and do this full time? Know it. You don't have to say it first — but don't walk into a negotiation without it.
